# Creative direction and editorial structure

## The idea

**One business. One connected way to run it.**

OfficeAdmin is not positioned as another dashboard. Its advantage is the continuity between the work and the records of that work. A takeoff becomes a linked estimate. A customer accepts the estimate. An invoice and payment become part of the books. Hours belong to the project. Documents and decisions stop living in isolated applications.

AIVA extends that continuity from records into conversations and follow-through. Its role in the story is not magical, unsupervised automation. It has project context, finds the relevant detail, prepares a useful draft, and moves forward after review.

The emotional payoff is **less chasing, more building**. The closing line is **Run the work. Not the runaround.**

## Audience and example

The intended viewer is a contractor or owner-operator who understands the work but spends too much time carrying information between tools. A six-station commercial EV installation is the demonstration, not a claim that the product only serves EV contractors.

Northline Electric, Cedar Court, Alex Lee, the project identifiers, documents, totals, and conversations are fictional. They provide continuity without exposing the owner's actual customers or books. The visible estimate sums to $18,450. The illustrative costing view uses $12,220 of direct cost, $6,230 of gross profit, and a rounded 33.8% gross margin. These are example project economics, not an ROI or performance claim.

## Editorial map

00:00–00:07 — The fragmentation. Floating notifications give way to a single point of focus. “One business. A thousand moving parts.”

00:07–00:15 — Product reveal. The authentic OfficeAdmin mark, a calm white stage, and a dimensional workspace reveal. “The business behind the work.”

00:15–00:24 — One project hub. Contacts, files, status, tasks, time, and financial context share a project. The product UI is a purpose-built illustration, not a capture of the live app.

00:24–00:34 — Plans to pricing. Six count markers and a traced conduit path become a linked estimate. The counted quantities remain connected. Pricing in the example is already supplied; the animation does not assert automatic price discovery or automatic AI takeoff.

00:34–00:43 — Clear customer approval. A proposal is sent, accepted in a customer-facing portal, then converted to an invoice. The actual workflow's separate send and conversion actions remain distinct.

00:43–00:52 — Field work. A crew schedule and a phone time clock show the same project. A clock-in starts a timer. The sequence emphasizes job-linked time and project reporting, not payroll or guaranteed geofence enforcement.

00:52–01:01 — Financial visibility. Invoice status, job costing, and a reviewed bank match connect operational work to the books. The gross-margin figures are illustrative.

01:01–01:09 — AIVA reveal. Dark stage, restrained purple, the actual robot mark, and a network of people, projects, email, documents, calendar, and conversations. The scene is visibly labeled private preview.

01:09–01:19 — Beyond transcription. A recorded commissioning conversation produces contextual next steps. Source context and human review are visible.

01:19–01:29 — Reviewed action. AIVA uses project sources to prepare a closeout draft. The draft is clearly unsent until the user approves. The illustration then shows a sent-and-saved state. No actual email was sent.

01:29–01:38 — The system, together. The film pulls out to the connected operating picture: customers, projects, estimates, invoices, documents, time, books, and inventory.

01:38–01:45 — The benefit. Two clean typographic beats: “Less chasing.” Then “More building.” The soundtrack deliberately leaves room for the statements.

01:45–01:52 — Brand and promise. OfficeAdmin + AIVA, “Run the work. Not the runaround.” A closing disclosure identifies illustrative UI, fictional demo data, private AIVA availability, and release-channel differences.

## Visual system

The actual OfficeAdmin blue is #0284c7. Stone-white backgrounds and deep navy alternate by narrative function. Purple is reserved for AIVA rather than used as a generic technology accent. Large, tightly tracked type carries the major ideas; interface information is subordinate. All essential claims are also in the narration, so the audience is not expected to read an entire application screen during a short shot.

Motion uses frame-based spring settling, drawn vector paths, gentle perspective changes, selective counter animation, staged interface states, and short overlapping transitions. No CSS timer animation, random per-render data, generated photography, or third-party commercial footage is used.

## Audio

The narration contains 160 words, with approximately 62 seconds of speech across the 112-second runtime. The remaining time is for recognition, transitions, music, and comprehension. The synthetic preset voice is intentionally calm and direct. AIVA is pronounced “Ava.”

The score is composed specifically for this timeline from synthesized pads, mallet-like tones, round bass, minimal percussion, and subtle filtered-air transitions. AIVA's entrance pulls back the pulse. The final promise opens into a major resolution. Interface confirmations have quiet, timed cues instead of exaggerated effects. The mixed master targets approximately -16 LUFS integrated and a -1.5 dBTP ceiling.
